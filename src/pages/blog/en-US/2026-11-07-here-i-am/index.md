Young people are always the new, and they are always the most vivid, most passionate, and most alive presence of an era. In just one hundred years, China went from being poor and weak—the "sick man of East Asia"—to standing up, growing rich, and becoming strong. In the countless social transformations along the way, we can always see the figures of young people. We are those young people.

Nearly a hundred years ago, beside the desks before the smoke of war, there was me. Having seen the incompetence and frailty of old China, and with the unjust verdict of the Paris Peace Conference handed down, we could no longer tolerate imperialism's reckless trampling of us. With one shot of the October Revolution, Marxism entered China. Chinese young people, typified by Li Dazhao and Chen Duxiu, came into contact for the first time with an ideology that truly belonged to the proletariat; it was also at this time that the young Mao Zedong planted a seed of Marxism and proletarian revolution. It was young people—it was us—who pushed this ancient nation out of its cold air, gradually onto the correct and suitable path, and ultimately made China stand up.

Decades ago, amid twists and turns and groping forward, there was me. The newborn republic received the enthusiastic support of the people, but we were still in a backward state. The country's primary task at the time was to develop production and improve the economy and people's livelihoods. We young people once again shouldered the main beam of "boosting production"—in steel mills, in automobile factories, beside the bridges under construction, in front of newly discovered oilfields... For the country's development, we were willing to give our own hands, even at the cost of our own lives. Countless young people, carrying such faith, completed one seemingly impossible great undertaking after another—we did it, all of it. It was young people—it was us—who set the newborn republic on the right track, improved people's lives, and on such a solid economic foundation finally reached reform and opening up, making China rich.

To this day, in the great rejuvenation of the nation, there is me. After a hundred years, the baton of the era has finally been passed into our hands. We young people of the new era still keep that refusal-to-lose faith. As the era develops, contemporary Chinese youth hold fast to their original hearts, and in science and technology, knowledge, national defense, and many other fields, they have shown the world the style of "youthful China." When closed-source models were all the rage, Liang Wenfeng launched DeepSeek in one move, showing the world the quality and cost-effectiveness of Chinese open-source models; mathematicians Wang Hong and Deng Yu won the Fields Medal, pushing the mathematics world further forward; on the China-India border, as the Indian military kept provoking, Chen Xiangrong, barely in his twenties, sacrificed himself for his country—"clear love, only for China"... It was young people—it was us—who, through each person's unbroken efforts, gradually made China strong.

A hundred years ago, they cried out: "With the self of youth, create the family of youth, the nation of youth, the people of youth, the humanity of youth." Each generation has its own mission, each generation has its own Long March. Today, we can finally reply to them full of hope:

"The way China looks is the way youth looks;  
"The way I look is the way China looks!"
