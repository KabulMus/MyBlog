Youth is always what's new—young people are invariably the most vivid, most passionate, and most vital presence of any era. In the span of just one hundred years, China went from being poor and weak, the "sick man of East Asia," to standing up, growing prosperous, and becoming strong. In the countless social transformations along the way, we can always see the figures of the young. We are those young people.

Nearly a hundred years ago, beside the desks before the smoke of war, there was me. Having witnessed the incompetence and frailty of old China, and with the unjust verdict of the Paris Peace Conference handed down, we could no longer endure the imperialist powers trampling over us at will. With the salvo of the October Revolution, Marxism was introduced to China.[^1] Chinese youth—typified by Li Dazhao and Chen Duxiu—came into contact for the first time with an ideology that truly belonged to the proletariat; it was also at this time that the young Mao Zedong planted the seed of Marxism and proletarian revolution. It was the youth—it was us—who pushed this ancient nation to shake off its coldness, to gradually step onto the correct and suitable path, and ultimately, to make China stand up.

[^1]: **Improper arrangement**: Chronologically, the Russian October Revolution broke out on November 7, 1917, while the May Fourth Movement broke out on May 4, 1919.

Decades ago, amid twists and tentative explorations, there was me. The newborn republic received enthusiastic support from the people, but we were still in a backward state. The country's foremost task at the time was to develop production and improve the economy and people's livelihoods. We young people once again shouldered the main burden of "boosting production"—in steel mills, in automobile factories, beside newly built bridges, in front of newly discovered oilfields... For the country's development, we were willing to give our own hands, even at the cost of our lives. Carrying such conviction, countless young people completed one seemingly impossible feat after another—and we did it, all of it. It was the youth—it was us—who set the newborn republic on the right track, improved people's lives, and on such a solid economic foundation, finally reached reform and opening up, making China prosperous.

To this day, in the great rejuvenation of the nation, there is me. After a hundred years, the baton of the times has finally been passed into our hands. We youth of the new era still hold onto that unyielding conviction. As the times advance, contemporary Chinese youth stay true to themselves, showing the world the elegance of "youthful China" in science and technology, knowledge, national defense, and many other fields. When closed-source models were all the rage, Liang Wenfeng[^2] launched DeepSeek in one stroke, showing the world the quality and cost-effectiveness of China's open-source models; mathematicians Wang Hong and Deng Yu won the Fields Medal, driving the further development of the mathematics world; on the China-India border, as the Indian military kept provoking, Chen Xiangrong, barely in his twenties, sacrificed himself for his country—"Clear love, only for China"... It was the youth—it was us—who, through one person's efforts after another, gradually made China strong.

[^2]: **Improper example**: Liang Wenfeng was born in 1985 and, as of the day this was written (November 7, 2026), is already 41 years old. The age range for youth as defined in China's *Medium- and Long-Term Youth Development Plan (2016–2025)* is 14 to 35 years old, so in the strict sense he can no longer be considered youth.

A hundred years ago, they cried out: "With the self of youth, create a family of youth, a nation of youth, a people of youth, a humanity of youth." Each generation has its own mission; each generation has its own long march. Today, we can finally reply to them, full of hope:

> What China looks like is what youth looks like;  
> What I look like is what China looks like!
